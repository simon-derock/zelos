Zelos

Every organ-on-a-chip image, certified.

An evidence-gated QC agent. It finds the imaging fault, names the lab fix, cites open literature, and blocks every claim the evidence cannot support.

Philip Simon Derock · v1.1 final-round build · Research tool, not a medical device
Hello, I am Philip, and this is Zelos. In one sentence: Zelos looks at an organ-on-a-chip image, tells you whether you can trust it, says what went wrong and what to do about it, and refuses to say anything its evidence cannot back up. I will show you what works, what does not, and what I corrected since the submission.
The problem

Images become datasets. Datasets become digital twins.

Bad frames corrupt data silently

A defocused, saturated, vignetted or bubble-filled frame looks like any other file. Once it is in a dataset, nobody knows it is there.

Invented evidence corrupts decisions silently

A language model asked to explain a bad frame can invent a number or a citation, and it sounds just as confident when it does.

Labs image hundreds of frames per experiment and cannot review them all. The question is not whether to automate the check, but whether the check can be trusted.

Organ-on-a-chip labs image their cultures every day, and those images become datasets and, increasingly, inputs to digital twins. A bad frame does not announce itself, and neither does a language model that makes up a citation. Both fail silently, and both end up inside the asset. So the check has to be automatic and it has to be auditable.
What Zelos outputs

Every image gets a certificate.

Verdict, route, cause, lab action, cited basis, claim ledger. Real pipeline output (web/public/data/site.json); images from Movcana et al., Data 2024, shared under CC BY / CC BY-SA. The FAIL frame is an injected fault on a real image; the cited passage is retrieved context, and the rule's own literature basis is shown as the amber line.
This is what comes out: a verdict, a route that says what to do next, the named cause, the lab action, the passage it cites, and a ledger that lists every sentence with the tool output or passage behind it. The amber line on the left is deliberate: for that cause our corpus does not support the action, and the certificate says so.
Architecture

The LLM proposes.
Tools compute.
The gate disposes.

The verdict, cause and action never depend on the LLM. If the model is wrong, slow or absent, the certificate is still correct; it is only less fluent.
The design rule is the headline: the physics tools compute the verdict, the cause and the action with no language model involved, and the model only proposes sentences as typed claims, in at most 2 calls. A deterministic gate then checks every number against a tool output and every citation against a retrieved passage, repairs once, and blocks what it cannot support. That is why the model is swappable, and I will show you that it is.

Result D · the shift finding

Absolute thresholds do not transfer between imaging sessions.

Unseen-session estimate, within targetabove targetTest sessionsTarget
  • Calibrated absolute cutoffs miss the 10% false-alarm target across unseen sessions: 21.3% and 17.6%. Session-relative scoring meets it: 9.7%.
  • In the upper fifth of sessions single-image scoring flags 28.4% of good images, session-relative 13.3%.
  • On the 39 test sessions the absolute modes were conservative (1.1% false flags) and found only 4.2% of expert-bad images. The pre-registered z=3 rule flags 64.7% of expert-good.
So Zelos scores each image against its own imaging session. Single-image mode remains available, and it is the weaker mode.
This is the finding that changed the design: fixed thresholds do not transfer across imaging sessions. When we leave one whole session out, the absolute cutoffs flag 21.3% and 17.6% of good images against a 10% target, and in the worst fifth of sessions it is 28.4%. Scoring each image relative to its own session gives 9.7%, which is why the session-relative mode is the default and single-image mode is the weaker option.

4 of 9 actions are literature-backed.
The other 5 say so.

SupportedUnsupported
  • Supported: defocus or blur, uneven illumination, low contrast, air bubble.
  • Unsupported in our 48-paper corpus: sensor noise, over-exposure, under-exposure, debris, compression artefacts. Guidance, not citations.
  • Threshold raised from the gate default 0.30 to 0.50 after reading the matches: off-topic sentences had passed for over-exposure (0.333), debris (0.375). Post hoc, disclosed.
  • Retrieval: MRR 1.000, 12/12 off-topic questions declined; the gold set is optimistic.
Every lab action in the rule table is looked up in our open literature corpus, sentence by sentence. 4 of the 9 are supported by a real sentence; 5 are not, and the certificate says so rather than citing something loosely related. I have to disclose one thing: the default support threshold, 0.30, let off-topic sentences through, so I raised it to 0.50 after reading the matches. That is post hoc, it is in the results file, and I am telling you here.
Reproducibility and engineering

Built to be checked, not trusted.

CI on every push

Lint, types, security scan, tests, an offline smoke run with provenance check, web build and Docker build.

Offline replay

Every Cohere and Workers AI response used in the results is committed. make judge needs no keys and no GPU.

Provenance check

A number in the README that is not in results/ fails CI. This deck and its Q&A are generated the same way.

Agent-native

CLI, FastAPI, an MCP server and a SKILL.md, so any MCP-capable agent can certify images.

Docker and Space

One container serves the upload demo: PNG, JPEG or TIFF up to 25 MB and 40 MP, processed in memory, 20 requests per minute per client.

CPU only

168 ms per image (p50) on a CPU. No training, no fine-tuning, no GPU.

Everything here is built to be re-run. Continuous integration runs on every push, every model response that backs a published number is committed and replayed offline, and a provenance check fails the build if a number in the README is not in the results files. The tools are exposed through a command line, an API, an MCP server and a skill file, and there is a Docker image and an upload demo with strict size limits. A full inspection takes 168 milliseconds on a CPU.
Honest limitations

What this does not show.

  • Cross-session shift: single-image absolute mode misses the false-alarm target on the upper fifth of unseen sessions.
  • Cause attribution is imperfect: blur and under-exposure are sometimes read as low contrast.
  • Illumination, debris and motion-blur faults are mostly missed at severity 3 and below.
  • The expert label is not an artifact label; the culture-state kNN uses expert-labelled neighbours.
  • Small, single-lab data: one public dataset, one microscope, 59 sessions.
  • Faults are injected by us; real-world fault prevalence is unknown.
  • Retrieval gold set was written from the corpus: optimistic.
  • Grounding checks are lexical; semantic entailment is future work.
  • Small samples: n=60 base images; n=20 per vision-LLM group; 38 grounding cases.
  • 5 of 9 lab actions have no literature basis in the corpus.
  • Certificates are content hashes, not cryptographic signatures.
  • Research tool. No clinical use, no diagnostic claims.
Here is the list I would rather you hear from me. The shift problem is not fully solved in single-image mode, cause naming is imperfect, 3 fault kinds are mostly missed, and the expert label is not an image-fault label. The data is one public dataset from one lab, the faults are ones I injected, several samples are small, and 5 of the 9 actions have no literature support in our corpus. Certificates are hashes, not signatures, and this is a research tool, not a medical device.

Roadmap

What I would do next.

Sponsor-aligned

Neural OoC and MEA modality

The same certificate for electrophysiology and calcium-imaging traces, so data assets carry one standard of trust.

Data

Longitudinal wells

Score each well against its own history rather than one frame, which is where drift and culture state live.

Trust

Entailment in the gate

Replace lexical support with a semantic entailment check, the largest known weakness of the gate.

Evidence

Larger corpus

Re-run rule citations over a bigger corpus when the API quota resets, aimed at the 5 unsupported actions.

Validation

Prospective study

Certify a partner lab's frames as they are acquired and compare with their own rejection decisions.

Integration

Instrument hook

Run on acquisition so a failing frame is re-imaged while the plate is still on the stage.

Next steps, in the order I would take them. The sponsor works on neural organ-on-a-chip data, so the first step is to extend the certificate to those modalities. Then longitudinal monitoring per well, an entailment model in the gate, a bigger corpus once the quota resets, and a prospective validation with a partner lab, which is the only thing that can show real-world benefit.
